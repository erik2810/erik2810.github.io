Backpropagating through a physics solver

Inverse structural design as gradient descent, in PyTorch

Erik Löffelholz · M.Sc. Mathematical Physics

01 The problem

Engineering optimisation treats the simulator as a black box

Pick a design, simulate, score, adjust, repeat.

To learn how $N$ parameters affect the objective, every black-box method re-simulates on the order of $N$ times per step: finite differences, CMA-ES, random search.

Structural design spaces have $N$ in the tens of thousands to millions. That is how one campaign becomes weeks of cluster time.

02 The contract change

A differentiable simulator returns all $N$ sensitivities in one backward pass

$$\nabla_\rho C = \text{one backward pass} \qquad \text{vs.} \qquad N{+}1 \text{ re-simulations}$$

Reverse-mode automatic differentiation is the adjoint method, rediscovered. Its cost does not depend on $N$.

03 The model

The canonical topology optimisation problem

A clamped elastic truss under a tip load. Per-node density $\rho \in (0,1)$ maps to stiffness by SIMP: $k(\rho) = k_{\min} + (k_{\max} - k_{\min})\rho^3$.

$$K(\rho)\,u = f, \qquad C = f \cdot u$$

Minimise compliance $C$ at a fixed material budget. This is how production structural optimisation is actually posed.

04 The one design decision

Solve through torch.linalg.solve

PyTorch knows how to differentiate the linear solve. The adjoint sensitivity that people derive by hand,

$$\partial C / \partial \rho_e = -u_e^{\top} (\partial K_e / \partial \rho_e)\, u_e,$$

is exactly what autograd computes. Nobody ever writes it down, and new objectives need no re-derivation.

05 In nine lines 
import torch
from ddtwin.geometry import Sheet
from ddtwin.physics import solve_static

sheet = Sheet.cantilever(rows=6, cols=10)
density = torch.full((sheet.num_nodes,), 0.4, requires_grad=True)
result = solve_static(sheet, density)
result.compliance.backward()   # d(compliance)/d(density), one pass
print(result.compliance.item(), density.grad.shape)   # scalar, (60,)

09 Honest caveats

What the numbers do not say

CMA-ES finds a slightly lower loss at these toy sizes. Evolution strategies are good at small, cheap problems. The solve economics is what kills them at scale, not their search quality.

And this is a truss FEM, not a continuum shell. The physics is deliberately the simplest thing that makes the cost structure measurable.

10 Where this goes

The same contract, everywhere

Any simulator you can differentiate becomes an optimiser: topology optimisation, system identification, digital twins that fit themselves to sensor data.

The compilation side of the argument (jit, vmap) lives in a companion JAX engine with its own measured benchmarks.
